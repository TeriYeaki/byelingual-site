Privacy

Byelingual Privacy Policy

Effective date: 9 November 2026

In short

To build a card, Byelingual sends your selected text through its server to OpenAI. Your phone removes chat sender names and masks detected links, email addresses, long numbers, codes and some address numbers, but it does not detect names written inside message text and can miss sensitive details, so check the text before sending. OpenAI does not train its models on API data by default and may keep prompts and outputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring. Byelingual’s server database keeps no readable text from your card requests: while a card is built, its output waits there encrypted with a key only your phone keeps, and is deleted within about a day. Request, usage and cost records are deleted after 90 days, and the random install record after 90 days without use. Your Library stays on your phone, unless you share, copy or save a card, and may be included in your phone’s backup. If you enable card-ready notifications on iPhone, Byelingual stores an Apple push token with the install record and asks Apple to deliver a fixed alert that contains no card text or sender name.

1. Who we are

Byelingual is a reading-practice app operated by its sole founder, Tharith Yeak, in Australia. In this policy, “Byelingual”, “we”, “us” and “our” mean the operator of the Byelingual app.

Byelingual has no user accounts. The app lets you paste or type text in a language you are learning and request an AI-generated “gloss card”.

Byelingual is in a beta test. For now, the iPhone app is available only through Apple’s TestFlight to people we invite, and cards are built on our test server, which works as this policy describes.

For privacy questions or requests, contact:

  • Email: support@byelingual.app
  • Service: Byelingual
  • Location: Australia

2. The short version

  • Your original text in the box, chat sender names, timestamps, Library, settings and remembered senders are stored on your phone. What leaves it is the selected, prepared text you send to build a card, the install, request and notification details described in section 4, and anything you choose to share, copy or save.
  • To build a card, the app sends the selected, prepared text to Byelingual’s server, which sends it to OpenAI.
  • Before sending, the phone removes structured chat sender names and masks detected links, email addresses, long numbers, codes and some address numbers. It does not detect names written inside message text. Masking can miss sensitive details, so check the selected text before sending.
  • Byelingual does not use pasted text for advertising, sell it or use it to train a Byelingual model.
  • OpenAI does not use API data to train its models by default unless the API customer opts in. Under default controls, it may keep prompts and responses for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring.
  • Byelingual’s server database keeps no readable text from your card requests. Request, usage and cost records are deleted after 90 days; a random install record is deleted after 90 days without use.
  • While a card is built, its output waits on the server encrypted, so you can pick it up if you leave. It is deleted when the server receives your phone’s confirmation that it has it, or within about a day; extra translations and details within about 25 hours. The key stays on your phone, never in the server database.
  • If you enable card-ready notifications on iPhone, Byelingual stores an Apple push token with your random install record. The alert contains no card text or sender name.

3. Information that stays on your phone

Text and Library

The app processes the original text on your phone. This includes text you type, paste, share from another app or capture with Scan Text, any chat structure it recognises, sender names, timestamps, links and the messages you select. The original raw text is not sent as a separate field.

Text shared from another app is placed in the text box and handled like a paste. On supported iPhones, Scan Text uses Apple’s VisionKit to recognise text from the camera on the phone. Byelingual receives the recognised text, not the camera images. Byelingual does not receive, store or send the camera images. If you later build a card, the recognised text is handled like other text as described below.

The text in the box, your cards (with the original text you chose, their phrases, readings, meanings and translations), favourites, remembered sender names and languages, your “which one is you” choices, reading, voice and display settings, and any practice history are stored in the app’s local database. While a card is being built, the phone also keeps the key that unlocks its held output. A card also stores its source on the phone: a Scan Text card is labelled “Scanned”, while text shared from another app is treated as pasted. Byelingual does not operate an account-based cloud Library.

Your phone’s operating system may include the local Library in a device or cloud backup. Apple, Google or your device provider controls those backups under its own settings and policies.

Building or deleting a card does not clear the text box. Deleting a card does not delete remembered senders or practice history.

Using Delete everything removes from that phone your Library, the text in the box, remembered senders and chat choices, cards still being built (it also asks the server to cancel them) and any practice history. It keeps your language, voice and display settings and the install identity, but turns off Show who sent it. It does not delete copies you shared, copied or saved, or information already held in an operating-system backup, Byelingual’s limited server records (including any stored push token) or OpenAI abuse-monitoring logs. While notifications stay on, the app keeps registering its token, including after Delete everything. To remove the stored token, turn notifications off too: the app asks the server to forget it the next time you open the app and tries again after the app is next restarted if that request fails. Uninstalling the app normally removes its local data, subject to operating-system backups; on iPhone, the install identity in secure storage can outlast an uninstall. Uninstalling leaves the server’s push token until Apple reports it invalid or the inactive-install clean-up described below removes it.

Install identity

The app creates a random install ID and a random install secret. They are not your Apple advertising identifier, name, email address or phone number. The secret is stored using the phone’s secure storage. A backup restored to a different phone is designed to receive a new install identity.

We use the install identity to authenticate requests, prevent abuse, enforce daily limits and avoid charging the same card request twice.

Sharing, copying, saving and listening

If you choose to share or copy a card, its text, readings and meanings go to the app or clipboard you choose. A shared or saved card image also shows the sender’s name when Show who sent it is on; Copy text and Text only leave it out. If you save a card image, the app writes it to your Photos using the operating system’s add-only permission; it does not read or upload your existing photos. These copies are outside Byelingual, and Delete everything does not reach them.

When you play a card aloud, its text goes to your phone’s built-in speech voices, not to Byelingual’s server.

4. Information sent to Byelingual’s server

Card building

When you choose to build a card, the app sends:

  • a random request ID;
  • the selected, cleaned and masked text;
  • the language of the text and the language in which meanings should be shown;
  • any languages, and their scripts, you asked to translate the card into;
  • the random install ID and install secret for authentication and limits, and a shared app key built into every copy of the app;
  • the app version; and
  • when pick-up is supported, a temporary encryption key used by the running server process to encrypt held output. The key is not written to the database or application logs.

If you later request more translations or details, the app sends the masked text again with the relevant phrases (their identifiers, positions, text and meanings), the requested languages and scripts, whether details were requested, and, when pick-up is supported, the card build’s temporary encryption key again.

To pick up, confirm or cancel a build, the app sends the random request ID and, when needed, a run number and the next encrypted-output sequence number. To show the daily count in Settings, it sends a request with no text content. These requests contain the random install identity and app version but no pasted or recognised text. Some of them, such as asking the server to forget a notification token, can happen before you first agree to send text; they create or refresh the random install record but carry no text.

The app does not deliberately add structured chat sender names, timestamps, unselected messages or the separate raw-text field to these requests. However, a person’s name, a date, health information or any other detail written inside a selected message can still be sent.

The app does not send the card’s “Typed”, “Pasted”, “Scanned” or “Chat” source label to the server. Scan Text sends no camera image; only recognised text can later be included in a card request after you choose to build it.

Card-ready notifications

On iPhone, Byelingual offers the system notification permission from Notify me when it’s ready while a long card is building. If you allow it, the app sends its Apple Push Notification service (APNs) device token and whether the token is for Apple’s development or production service to Byelingual’s server, together with the random install identity and app version. The server stores the token, environment and registration time with the install record. While notifications are on, the app keeps registering its current token, including after Delete everything. Whenever the app opens with notifications off, it asks the server once during that opening to forget the token; if the request fails, it tries again after the app is next restarted.

When a card finishes, Byelingual may give Apple the device token, the random card request ID and a fixed alert titled “Your card is ready”. The notification contains no card text, language, meaning or sender name. Apple processes this information to deliver the notification.

What masking covers

The current on-device rules mask:

  • detected web links and email addresses;
  • runs containing at least six digits, including many phone, account and ID numbers;
  • certain tokens of at least six characters that mix Latin letters and digits, as codes do; and
  • certain Chinese and English street, floor, block, room or unit numbers.

Common dates and times, prices, counts, short numbers, many address formats and names written inside message text may remain readable. Detection can also fail. Check the selected text and remove anything you do not want Byelingual and OpenAI to process.

Only submit text that you are allowed to use. Do not submit passwords, authentication codes, payment-card details, government identifiers, highly sensitive information, confidential work material or private information about another person without an appropriate right or permission.

5. How we use information

We use the information described above to:

  • generate and deliver the gloss card and requested extras;
  • validate the model output against the text sent;
  • let a card be picked up after you leave the app;
  • authenticate an app installation and apply the daily limit;
  • register and deliver card-ready notifications you enable;
  • prevent duplicate requests, abuse and unexpected spending;
  • measure request size, model use and cost;
  • diagnose failures, monitor service health, maintain security and improve reliability;
  • provide customer support when you contact us; and
  • meet legal obligations and protect users, Byelingual and others.

We do not use your text for targeted advertising, create advertising profiles, sell it or share it with data brokers. The app contains no advertising SDK or cross-app tracking system.

6. Service providers and disclosure

We give providers only the information needed for their role.

Supabase

Supabase provides Byelingual’s hosted database, Edge Function and server infrastructure, logs and backups. The test server’s database is in AWS’s ap-southeast-1 region in Singapore; its server functions may run in another Supabase region, usually the one nearest you. The phone sends card requests to this infrastructure before any model request is made. Supabase describes customer application data as data it generally processes for its customer under a data-processing addendum. See Supabase’s privacy policy.

Supabase keeps its own platform logs for 7 days. They record requests to our server, including the IP address they came from but not what the requests contain, and our server functions’ and database’s log lines. Supabase also keeps a daily backup of our database for 7 days; it holds the same records described under Retention, including output still waiting encrypted for pick-up but never the key that unlocks it, so a record deleted from the database can stay in a backup for up to 7 more days.

OpenAI

OpenAI generates the gloss card. Byelingual sends OpenAI the selected masked text, instructions and language or task settings needed to create the response. The OpenAI request does not include your Byelingual install ID, install secret, chat sender field or raw-text field.

Byelingual sets store: false on these API requests. This prevents ordinary Responses API application-state storage, but it does not by itself provide Zero Data Retention. Under OpenAI’s default API controls:

  • API inputs and outputs are not used to train or improve OpenAI models unless the API customer explicitly opts in;
  • abuse-monitoring logs may contain prompts, responses and related metadata; and
  • those logs are normally kept for up to 30 days, unless longer retention is required by law or reasonably necessary to protect OpenAI’s services or another person from harm.

OpenAI says Modified Abuse Monitoring and Zero Data Retention are available only to approved eligible customers and require more configuration. See OpenAI’s API data controls and OpenAI’s privacy policy.

Apple and Google

Apple distributes the iPhone app under Apple’s privacy policy. During the beta it does this through TestFlight, which shares with us the name and email address you were invited with, your device model and iOS version, how often the app was installed, opened and crashed, crash reports, and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send through TestFlight. Apple describes this in the TestFlight app. We use it only to run the beta and fix problems. If a screenshot you send shows someone else’s message, we don’t copy it anywhere else: we describe the problem in our own words.

If you enable card-ready notifications, Apple also processes the APNs device token, random request ID and fixed alert described above to deliver the notification. When the Android app launches, Google will perform a similar distribution role under Google’s privacy policy.

Byelingual has no purchases or subscriptions. If we add them, we will update this policy first.

Other disclosures

We may disclose information when reasonably necessary to comply with law or valid legal process; investigate fraud, abuse or security incidents; protect legal rights or safety; or complete a merger, financing, acquisition or sale of the service. If ownership changes, we will require the recipient to handle personal information consistently with this policy and give notice where required.

We do not permit service providers to use card content for their own advertising. We require providers acting for us to protect data appropriately and use it only for the contracted service, subject to their legal obligations.

7. Retention

Different information has different retention periods.

How long information is kept
Information Retention
Raw paste, sender names, timestamps and unselected text On the phone until you clear or replace the text, use Delete everything or uninstall, subject to device backups. Building or deleting a card does not clear it. A card keeps the original text you chose for it until you delete the card.
Library, remembered senders, practice history and settings On the phone, subject to device backups. Cards stay until you delete them; remembered senders and chat choices until you forget them in Settings or use Delete everything; practice history until Delete everything; language, voice and display settings until you uninstall.
Readable request text in Byelingual’s server process Used in memory to build the card, and not written to the Byelingual database or application logs. Supabase’s platform logs record that a request happened, not its contents.
Encrypted held card output Held from the start of a build. A card’s output is deleted when your phone confirms it has the card or cancels it, or otherwise after about 24 hours. Extra translations and details are deleted by an hourly clean-up about 24 hours after they finish, so within about 25 hours, even after your phone has them.
Install record Kept while the app is in use. An inactive install record is removed by the daily clean-up after about 90 days without use, once its request rows have also reached their 90-day deletion point. This removes the install UUID, secret hash, creation timestamp, last-seen timestamp and any APNs token, environment and token-registration timestamp stored with them. If the app is used again later, it registers as a new record. Turning notifications off removes the stored push token from the server the next time the app is opened; a failed request is tried again after the app is next restarted. Delete everything alone does not remove it while notifications stay on. Uninstalling leaves it until Apple reports it invalid or this inactive-install clean-up removes it.
Request, usage and cost records The request UUID, install UUID, day, state or outcome, text size and phrase estimates, model, route and prompt versions, each model attempt, timestamps, token counts and cost. A daily clean-up removes them once their day (in UTC) is more than 90 days old: the build’s day for a card and its model attempts, and the extras request’s own day for extra translations and details. A request stays until the records that depend on it are removed. They do not contain the pasted text.
Byelingual application or platform logs and backups Application logs are designed not to contain pasted text, camera images, pick-up keys or push tokens. They can contain request IDs, a hashed install ID, the app version, text size and phrase counts, model, route and prompt versions, timings, outcomes, token counts and cost. Push-related log entries can contain a hashed install ID, random request ID, app version, registration or delivery result, HTTP status and APNs error reason. Supabase keeps these logs, and its request logs with IP addresses, for 7 days. Daily database backups are kept for 7 days, so a deleted record can stay in a backup for up to 7 more days; held output in a backup stays encrypted, and its key is never in the database or its backups.
OpenAI API abuse-monitoring logs Under default controls, up to 30 days, with the limited legal and harm exceptions described above.
Support email Deleted within 12 months of our last reply, unless we still need it to fix the problem or the law requires us to keep it.

We may preserve information for longer if reasonably necessary for a legal claim, security incident, fraud prevention or legal obligation. When that exception ends, we will delete or de-identify the information unless another valid reason applies.

8. Security

Byelingual uses data minimisation, on-device processing, secure device storage for the install secret, encrypted network connections, restricted database access, input and output validation, and limited application logging. Held card output is encrypted using AES-256-GCM with a fresh nonce for each line. The server database does not store the key; your phone does.

The running server process temporarily receives the pick-up key so it can encrypt output. Saying “only your phone can read a held card” means that the stored database rows cannot be read without the key retained by the phone. It does not mean the server process never handled the key or plaintext while generating the card.

No internet or storage system is completely secure. Avoid submitting information whose disclosure could cause serious harm.

9. International processing

Byelingual is operated from Australia. The test server’s database is in AWS’s ap-southeast-1 region in Singapore, and its server functions may run in another Supabase region, usually the one nearest you. Supabase, OpenAI, Apple, Google, Cloudflare and their subprocessors may also process information in other countries, including the United States. Those countries may have different privacy laws.

During the beta, we invite only testers who live outside the European Union, European Economic Area and United Kingdom, and when Byelingual reaches the App Store, its first storefronts will exclude them too. Where applicable law requires it, we will use appropriate contractual and transfer safeguards and assess providers before sending personal information overseas.

10. Your choices and privacy requests

You control whether to submit text. Before the first cloud card build, the app explains the OpenAI processing and asks you to choose Continue or Not now. You can edit the selection, remove private details, choose Not now or avoid the cloud card feature.

After permission is given, you can turn off Settings › Privacy › Send text to build cards. Turning it off stops future cloud card builds and the app asks again before another build. It does not undo completed processing or immediately erase OpenAI logs subject to its retention rules.

You can delete local items individually or use Delete everything for the local data listed in its confirmation screen. Because there is no account, local deletion does not automatically identify or erase server records.

You may email support@byelingual.app to ask what personal information we hold about you, request correction or deletion, object to or restrict processing where applicable, withdraw consent where processing relies on consent, or ask for a portable copy where the law provides that right. We may need enough information to verify the request and locate the relevant record. Some rights have legal exceptions.

Because there is no account, our server records are linked only to a random install ID that the app does not show you, so we usually cannot tell which records are yours. A daily clean-up deletes request and usage records after 90 days, and an install record after about 90 days without use. If you write to us, we will tell you what we can find and delete what we can.

If Australian privacy law applies to our handling and you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner. If EU or UK data-protection law applies, you may also complain to the data-protection authority where you live or work. EU or UK rights can include access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection and portability, depending on the circumstances.

11. Legal bases for EU/UK processing

Byelingual is not offered in the European Union, European Economic Area or United Kingdom. This section applies only if Byelingual is intentionally offered there in the future.

If this section applies, we rely on:

  • contract necessity to process the user’s selected text and provide a requested card or purchase;
  • legitimate interests to authenticate installations, apply fair-use limits, prevent fraud or abuse, keep the service secure, diagnose failures and operate a reliable paid service, after balancing those interests against people’s rights;
  • legal obligation for records or disclosures required by tax, accounting, consumer or other law; and
  • consent where a particular optional activity or applicable law requires it, which can be withdrawn for future processing.

Text supplied by a user can contain personal or special-category information about somebody else. Contract necessity with the user does not automatically cover that other person, and special-category data may require an additional legal condition.

12. Children

Byelingual is not directed to children under 16. People under 16 must not use Byelingual. We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from a child under 16.

Language-learning apps can appeal to teenagers, and an app-store age rating is not proof of age.

During the beta, we invite only testers aged 16 or over.

If you believe a child has submitted information, email support@byelingual.app. We will investigate and delete information where required and reasonably possible. Because pasted text may concern someone other than the user, do not paste private or sensitive information about a child.

13. Customer support

If you contact us, we receive the email address or other contact details you use, your message and any material you choose to attach. We use it to respond, investigate the issue, keep an appropriate support record and protect the service. Do not send a full private conversation when a smaller example will do.

Your email provider and ours process the email under their own terms. Mail to support@byelingual.app is received by Cloudflare’s Email Routing and forwarded to our Gmail inbox, which Google provides, where we keep it as the Retention section says. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy and Google’s privacy policy.

14. Policy website

15. Contact

Questions, complaints and privacy requests can be sent to support@byelingual.app. Write “Byelingual privacy” in the subject line and include only the information needed to handle the request.

16. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app, providers or law changes. We will publish the new version at https://teriyeaki.github.io/byelingual-site/privacy/ and change the effective date. If a change materially affects how previously collected personal information is used, we will provide a prominent in-app notice and request new permission where required before the new use begins.
